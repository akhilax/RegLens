## Section 3 — Beneficial owner {#sec3-bo}
Where the customer is a company, the beneficial owner is the natural person who, alone or together, has a controlling ownership interest (ownership of more than the prescribed percentage of shares, capital or profits — verify the current threshold) or exercises control through other means. Banks must identify the beneficial owner and take reasonable measures to verify their identity.

## Section 15 — Customer Due Diligence (CDD) {#sec15-cdd}
Regulated entities must undertake customer due diligence while establishing an account-based relationship, while carrying out any international money transfer, and when there is a suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing. CDD includes identifying the customer, verifying identity using officially valid documents, obtaining the PAN or Form 60, and understanding the nature of business.

## Section 36 — Ongoing due diligence {#sec36}
Regulated entities must undertake ongoing due diligence to ensure that a customer's transactions are consistent with their knowledge of the customer, the customer's business and risk profile, and the source of funds. Large and complex transactions, transactions with unusual patterns, and transactions exceeding thresholds prescribed for specific categories of accounts warrant close monitoring.

## Section 37 — Risk categorisation {#sec37}
Customers must be categorised as low, medium or high risk based on an assessment of risk, considering identity, social or financial status, nature of business activity, and information about the customer's business and location. Risk categorisation must be reviewed periodically.

## Section 38 — Periodic updation of KYC {#sec38}
Periodic updation of KYC must be carried out at least once every two years for high-risk customers, once every eight years for medium-risk customers and once every ten years for low-risk customers, from the date of opening of the account or the last KYC updation. Where updation is overdue, the regulated entity should seek updated documents and may impose partial freezing after due notice.

## Section 39 — Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) {#sec39-edd}
Enhanced due diligence must be applied to higher-risk customers, including non-face-to-face customers, customers from jurisdictions identified as high-risk, and complex or unusually large transactions. EDD includes obtaining additional information on source of funds and wealth, senior management approval, and enhanced ongoing monitoring.

## Section 41 — Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) {#sec41-pep}
Politically exposed persons are individuals entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign country, and regulated entities should apply equivalent measures to domestic PEPs as per their policy. For PEPs, regulated entities must gather sufficient information, verify source of funds, obtain senior management approval for establishing or continuing the relationship, and subject the account to enhanced ongoing monitoring.

## Section 55 — Jurisdictions not applying FATF recommendations {#sec55-fatf}
Regulated entities must give special attention to business relationships and transactions with persons (including legal persons and financial institutions) from or in countries that do not or insufficiently apply the FATF Recommendations and jurisdictions included in FATF statements. The background and purpose of such transactions must be examined, findings documented, and enhanced due diligence applied.

## Section 64 — Wire transfers {#sec64-wire}
All cross-border wire transfers must be accompanied by accurate and meaningful originator and beneficiary information. The beneficiary bank must have risk-based procedures to identify wire transfers lacking required originator information and to decide whether to execute, reject or suspend them, and whether to file a suspicious transaction report.
