## Rule 2(1)(g) — Meaning of suspicious transaction {#rule2-1-g}
A suspicious transaction is any transaction, attempted or completed, whether or not made in cash, which to a person acting in good faith gives rise to a reasonable ground of suspicion that it may involve proceeds of an offence, appears to be made in circumstances of unusual or unjustified complexity, appears to have no economic rationale or bona fide purpose, or gives rise to a reasonable ground of suspicion that it may involve financing of terrorism. Splitting cash deposits into amounts just below a reporting or identification threshold (structuring) is a classic indicator of suspicion.

## Rule 3(1)(A) — Cash transactions above ₹10 lakh {#rule3-1-a}
Every reporting entity must maintain a record of all cash transactions of a value of more than ten lakh rupees or its equivalent in foreign currency. Such cash transactions are reported to the Director, FIU-IND, through the Cash Transaction Report (CTR).

## Rule 3(1)(B) — Integrally connected cash transactions {#rule3-1-b}
Every reporting entity must maintain a record of all series of cash transactions integrally connected to each other which have been individually valued below ten lakh rupees or its equivalent in foreign currency, where such series of transactions have taken place within a month and the monthly aggregate exceeds ten lakh rupees. These connected series are included in the monthly Cash Transaction Report.

## Rule 3(1)(BA) — Receipts by non-profit organisations {#rule3-1-ba}
Every reporting entity must maintain a record of all transactions involving receipts by non-profit organisations of value more than ten lakh rupees or its equivalent in foreign currency. These are reported through the Non-Profit Organisation Transaction Report (NTR).

## Rule 3(1)(C) — Counterfeit currency transactions {#rule3-1-c}
Every reporting entity must maintain a record of all cash transactions where forged or counterfeit currency notes or bank notes have been used as genuine, or where any forgery of a valuable security or document has taken place facilitating the transaction. These are reported through the Counterfeit Currency Report (CCR).

## Rule 3(1)(D) — Suspicious transactions {#rule3-1-d}
Every reporting entity must maintain a record of all suspicious transactions, whether or not made in cash and whether or not attempted. Suspicious transactions are reported to FIU-IND through the Suspicious Transaction Report (STR). The decision that a transaction is suspicious is taken by the Principal Officer after review; a rule alert alone is a candidate, not a determination.

## Rule 3(1)(E) — Cross-border wire transfers above ₹5 lakh {#rule3-1-e}
Every reporting entity must maintain a record of all cross-border wire transfers of the value of more than five lakh rupees or its equivalent in foreign currency, where either the origin or the destination of the funds is in India. These are reported through the Cross-Border Wire Transfer Report (CBWTR).

## Rule 6 — Retention of records {#rule6}
Records of transactions referred to in Rule 3 must be maintained for a period of five years from the date of the transaction between the client and the reporting entity. Records must allow reconstruction of individual transactions, including the nature, amount, currency, date and parties.

## Rule 7 — Procedure for furnishing information {#rule7}
The Designated Director and the Principal Officer of the reporting entity are responsible for furnishing information to the Director, FIU-IND. Information must be furnished in the prescribed electronic formats and the reporting entity must evolve an internal mechanism for detecting reportable transactions.

## Rule 8(1) — Timeline for CTR, NTR and CBWTR {#rule8-1}
Information in respect of transactions referred to in Rule 3(1)(A), (B), (BA) and (E) is to be furnished to the Director, FIU-IND, for every month by the fifteenth day of the succeeding month.

## Rule 8(2) — Timeline for STR {#rule8-2}
Information in respect of suspicious transactions referred to in Rule 3(1)(D) is to be furnished promptly, and not later than seven working days on being satisfied that the transaction is suspicious. The clock starts on the date of determination by the Principal Officer, not on the transaction date.

## Rule 8(3) — Timeline for CCR {#rule8-3}
Information in respect of counterfeit currency transactions referred to in Rule 3(1)(C) is to be furnished not later than seven working days from the date of occurrence of such transactions (verify the current timeline against the official notification).

## Rule 9 — Client due diligence {#rule9}
Every reporting entity must, at the time of commencement of an account-based relationship, identify its clients, verify their identity, obtain information on the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship, and determine whether a client is acting on behalf of a beneficial owner. Ongoing due diligence requires scrutiny of transactions to ensure they are consistent with the client's profile and source of funds.
